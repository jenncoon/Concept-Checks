Maya's Story Continues

Maya's business is booming. Sweet Rise Bakery loved her work, and now three more clients have signed on. But her accountant sat her down for a serious talk: "Maya, your business is growing faster than your controls. One mistake, one missing check, and things could go very wrong very fast."

Maya needs to build a proper internal control system. That is where you come in. For each COSO element, evaluate two options and decide which is stronger, or identify the gap in a control Maya already has.

End of Year One

Maya's income statement shows a profit. But her bank account feels emptier than she expected. Her accountant hands her the Statement of Cash Flows for the first time. "Maya," she says, "net income tells you what you earned. This tells you where the cash actually went."

Now an investor is looking at Maya's numbers. Help her understand what the statement reveals from an investor's perspective.

The Statement of Cash Flows: Three Windows Into the Business

SECTION 1
Operating
Day-to-day business. Cash from clients, cash out for rent, supplies, and payroll.
→
SECTION 2
Investing
Buying or selling long-term assets: equipment, vehicles, property.
→
SECTION 3
Financing
Borrowing, repaying loans, owner investments, or withdrawals.

All three sections together explain why cash changed from the start to the end of the period.
